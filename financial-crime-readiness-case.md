# Wells Fargo: Financial-Crime AI Evidence Readiness

**Experience summary | AI workflow enablement | Individual contribution within a broader program**

**Delivered contribution:** Sensitive-information preparation, evidence-readiness checks and a working HTML readiness prototype built with Copilot and refined with business experts and data-ingestion teams.

## Situation and business scale

The financial-crime operation handles approximately **125,000 transaction-monitoring alerts each month**. Around **97% are false positives**, creating substantial review work for more than **1,000 investigators**.

Investigators gathered information from multiple sources, reviewed spreadsheets manually and sometimes discovered missing or unusable evidence late in an investigation. Improving the downstream AI workflow depended on having usable evidence at the start.

## My task and actions

My contribution focused on two foundational capabilities: a case-masking assistant and a data-readiness analyzer. They prepare sensitive information for controlled AI use and check whether case packages contain complete, usable evidence before testing and analysis.

I built a working HTML readiness prototype with Copilot, then refined it with business experts and ingestion teams. It connects an aggregate view of ready and blocked cases to the reasons behind each status: missing information, extraction problems and masking issues.

The design makes the next action visible: what is blocking the case, why it matters and which team needs to resolve it. It also creates a reusable way to check readiness before consuming investigation capacity.

## Broader workflow and human accountability

The redesigned workflow uses AI to gather and analyze evidence, support escalation decisions and draft investigation narratives. Investigators retain responsibility for final decisions. Passing a readiness check means the evidence meets the agreed input requirements; it does not establish that an AI conclusion is correct.
